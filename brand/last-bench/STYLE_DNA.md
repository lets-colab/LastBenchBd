# Last Bench — Style DNA

Status: **canonical execution grammar**  
Approved direction: **28 September 2026**

This file translates the approved Last Bench brand references into repeatable art-direction rules for designers, agents, Canva/Figma work, campaign templates and web implementation.

It supplements:

- `BRAND_BLUEPRINT.md`
- `ART_DIRECTION.md`
- `LOGO_USAGE.md`
- `brand-lock.json`

It does not replace the logo source lock.

## 1. Signature visual composition

The approved Last Bench look is built from five recurring layers:

1. **Warm white / off-white trust field**
2. **Bold black editorial typography**
3. **Restrained Last Bench green progress accents**
4. **Topographic / route-line texture**
5. **A grounded black or photographic lower field**

The result should feel like an **education opportunity brand with editorial discipline**, not a generic consultancy flyer.

## 2. Surface hierarchy

### Light-led composition

Default for:

- university intake campaigns;
- admissions information;
- consultation posters;
- office walls;
- roll-up banners;
- ID cards;
- partner information.

Target visual balance:

- warm white / pale neutral: **55–75%**
- charcoal / black: **20–35%**
- Brand Green: **5–12%**
- gray / line texture: supporting only

Do not turn the entire composition green.

### Dark-led composition

Use for:

- event / community moments;
- premium back sides of badges/cards;
- cinematic campaign chapters;
- CTA/footer zones.

Keep Brand Green selective and high-signal.

## 3. Editorial typography system

### Headline behavior

Use bold, uppercase, stacked headlines.

Typical structure:

```
FROM
LAST BENCH.
TO MALAYSIA.
```

or

```
OCTOBER
INTAKE
2026
```

Rules:

- 2–4 lines preferred;
- one key word/phrase may turn green;
- black/charcoal is the default on light;
- white is the default on dark;
- line-height should feel tight and poster-like;
- avoid long centered paragraphs as headlines;
- no decorative type effects.

### Supporting copy

Use sentence case, high readability and short blocks.

The headline creates emotion.  
The body creates trust.

## 4. Logo placement grammar

Logo behavior is governed by `LOGO_USAGE.md`.

For layout:

- place the full logo in clear negative space;
- never crowd it against a headline, partner mark or crop;
- primary poster/roll-up placement is usually top or upper-middle;
- icon-only use is allowed for repeat patterns, lanyards, micro-signatures and compact navigation;
- partner/university identity stays separate.

The logo must remain transparent and contrast-adaptive.

## 5. Topographic contour language

Contour lines are a supporting texture, not the hero.

Use them:

- in corners;
- in black lower waves;
- behind ID-card edges;
- on lanyards;
- as low-opacity background detail;
- as a continuity device between print, spatial and digital surfaces.

Rules:

- thin line weight;
- low contrast;
- irregular organic spacing;
- green may appear on selected route/contour lines;
- never let texture reduce legibility.

Recommended opacity:
- light surface: **4–10%**
- dark surface: **7–14%**

## 6. The black wave / grounded lower field

A recurring approved composition device is an organic black lower field that rises in a smooth wave.

It can carry:

- CTA;
- QR code;
- contact line;
- proof/service information;
- dark contrast chapter;
- skyline transition.

Rules:

- use one or two smooth curves, not noisy blobs;
- edge may have one restrained green line;
- contour texture may sit inside the black field;
- do not let it cover more than needed;
- it should visually ground the composition.

## 7. Malaysia / journey visual language

For Malaysia-specific Education & Mobility work:

Approved:

- Kuala Lumpur skyline;
- Petronas Towers;
- KL Tower;
- route from Bangladesh to Malaysia;
- minimal map;
- airplane / location pin;
- campus photography;
- skyline line-art;
- realistic city photography.

Rules:

- Malaysia imagery supports the service lane; it does not become the parent identity;
- route language should be clean and directional;
- country/location labels should be functional, not decorative;
- use verified institution imagery when representing a specific university.

## 8. Skyline treatment

Two approved modes:

### Line-art skyline

Best for:

- office walls;
- diagrams;
- informational sections;
- white-background compositions.

Use thin charcoal/gray architectural linework.

### Real city photography

Best for:

- roll-up banners;
- campaign hero;
- emotional lower sections.

Grade toward:

- realistic daylight;
- neutral/warm balance;
- restrained saturation;
- crisp architectural detail.

Do not over-HDR or turn the skyline into fantasy imagery.

## 9. Service icon system

Use thin-line icons inside simple circular containers for:

- admissions;
- university selection;
- visa guidance;
- scholarship support;
- student community;
- consultation;
- documents;
- arrival support.

Rules:

- green stroke on light;
- white/green on dark;
- equal visual weight;
- consistent line thickness;
- labels beneath icons;
- no mixed icon families within one composition.

## 10. Journey route system

Approved route treatment:

`Bangladesh → motion / travel → Malaysia`

Use:

- thin dotted or dashed route;
- one simple aircraft symbol;
- minimal location markers;
- green as the directional cue.

Avoid large illustrated maps, flags everywhere, or tourist-poster styling.

## 11. CTA system

CTA must be visually obvious but integrated.

Preferred treatments:

### Light field
- black/charcoal text;
- green CTA emphasis;
- black or green button.

### Dark field
- white headline;
- green action phrase;
- QR or CTA module with sufficient quiet space.

For QR sections:

- keep QR deterministic and tested;
- use a clean high-contrast box;
- pair with one short action line;
- do not let contour texture intersect the QR quiet zone.

## 12. ID card / staff collateral

Approved ID-card language:

Front:
- warm-white field;
- black wordmark/logo treatment;
- green outline portrait ring;
- role/name hierarchy;
- subtle topographic corners;
- grounded Malaysia skyline at base.

Back:
- charcoal/black field;
- white wordmark;
- green bench-arrow;
- white/green tagline;
- concise service or contact information.

Lanyard:
- black base;
- repeated exact icon/wordmark;
- faint contour texture;
- restrained green route line.

## 13. Office / spatial graphics

Large-format wall graphics should feel architectural.

Use:

- large negative space;
- one dominant statement;
- central or grid-aligned full logo;
- route/map system;
- service icon row;
- skyline line-art;
- black organic lower wave;
- plants / physical environment should not be visually fought by the artwork.

Do not turn walls into giant brochures.

## 14. Roll-up banner grammar

Recommended top-to-bottom structure:

1. exact logo;
2. large headline;
3. journey route;
4. one short service promise;
5. compact service-icon row;
6. skyline / hero image;
7. dark CTA/footer;
8. QR + contact / website.

Keep the first 40% readable from distance.

## 15. Visual tone keywords

Use these as art-direction prompts:

- editorial
- architectural
- premium
- grounded
- optimistic
- global
- directional
- human
- minimal
- structured
- warm
- credible

Do not use as default:

- neon
- cyberpunk
- gaming
- glossy corporate
- maximalist
- playful cartoon
- generic SaaS
- discount flyer
- luxury gold
- tropical travel poster

## 16. Repetition rules

Consistency should come from repeating:

- black + warm white + restrained green;
- stacked bold typography;
- exact transparent logo;
- contour texture;
- journey line;
- skyline;
- black lower wave;
- thin circular icon system.

Do not create a new visual gimmick for every campaign.

## 17. Campaign adaptation

### University intake

Primary job: make the intake opportunity clear.

Use:
- verified university logo;
- intake month/year as dominant information;
- program/faculty information grouped cleanly;
- campus/address details where relevant;
- Last Bench CTA/support layer.

### Scholarship / offer

Primary job: clarify the benefit and eligibility.

Do not make the numerical offer visually louder than the institution/opportunity unless the campaign brief explicitly requires it.

### Consultation

Primary job: move the viewer to one action.

Use one CTA and a tested QR/URL.

## 18. Final visual test

A visual should pass all of these:

- Can the viewer identify Last Bench within 2–3 seconds?
- Is there one dominant message?
- Does the composition use negative space?
- Is green restrained and meaningful?
- Is the logo transparent and on the correct contrast variant?
- Are topographic lines secondary?
- Does the black field ground rather than dominate?
- Is Malaysia imagery contextual rather than brand-defining?
- Are icons consistent?
- Is the CTA singular and obvious?
- Are factual partner details verified?
- Does it still work at mobile/social size?

If not, revise before release.

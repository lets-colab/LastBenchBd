# Last Bench — Look, Feel & Art Direction

Status: **canonical visual direction**  
Applies to: public website, social media, posters, events, admissions campaigns, documents, decks, signage and digital product surfaces.

This document defines how Last Bench should **feel**, not just which colors to use. It must be read together with `BRAND_BLUEPRINT.md`, `LOGO_USAGE.md`, `brand-lock.json` and the visual reference boards in `reference/`.

## 1. Core visual idea

Last Bench is an opportunity accelerator built around forward movement.

The visual system should communicate:

- **possibility without hype**
- **trust without corporate stiffness**
- **ambition without visual noise**
- **progress without gimmicks**
- **human aspiration supported by structure**

The governing visual law is:

> **Dark for emotion. White for trust. Green for progress.**

Every composition should have one clear emotional job.

## 2. Brand personality in visual form

Last Bench should feel:

- cinematic;
- human;
- credible;
- forward-moving;
- globally aware;
- optimistic;
- structured;
- premium but accessible.

Avoid the generic study-abroad-consultancy look: crowded badges, random flags, excessive gradients, floating icons, fake university seals, overloaded copy and discount-led visual clutter.

## 3. Color system

### Brand Green — `#00C853`

Use for progress, CTA, active state, proof of movement, the bench-arrow mark and selected emphasis.

Do not flood the page with green. Green gains power from restraint.

### Bright Green — `#00E676`

Use selectively on dark backgrounds when extra luminosity is needed.

### Charcoal — `#111111`

Primary emotional/dark canvas. Use for cinematic hero sections, premium event surfaces and high-contrast storytelling.

### Warm White — `#FAFAF8`

Primary trust canvas. Use for documents, admissions information, detailed service communication and light campaign layouts.

### Sage — `#E6F2E9`

Secondary soft field for calm informational sections and supportive surfaces.

### Gray — `#6B6F76`

Secondary text and quiet system information.

## 4. Typography

### Display — General Sans

Use for:

- major campaign headlines;
- section titles;
- decisive statements;
- high-impact numbers.

The display voice should be bold, simple and confident.

### Body/UI — Sora

Use for:

- explanatory copy;
- labels;
- form fields;
- utility text;
- digital-product interfaces.

### Typography behavior

- short headlines;
- clean line breaks;
- strong hierarchy;
- generous breathing room;
- avoid decorative font mixing;
- avoid all-caps paragraphs;
- do not compress text simply to fit more content.

## 5. Logo behavior

The logo is not decoration. It is a locked identity asset.

See `LOGO_USAGE.md` for the complete rule.

At a glance:

- logo background is **always transparent**;
- light/white background -> black/charcoal wordmark;
- dark/black background -> white wordmark;
- bench + rising arrow stays Brand Green;
- `BENCHMARK` stays Brand Green;
- never add a black or white plate behind the logo just to force contrast;
- never let AI generate or redraw the logo.

## 6. Composition

### One dominant idea

Every poster, viewport or campaign frame should communicate one primary message before secondary information appears.

### Hierarchy order

Typical campaign hierarchy:

1. brand / partner context;
2. primary opportunity or announcement;
3. date / intake / event;
4. one dominant CTA;
5. supporting facts;
6. operational details.

### Spacing

Use generous negative space. Do not fill empty areas simply because they are available.

### Alignment

Prefer strong grid-based alignment. Intentional asymmetry is acceptable when it improves movement or cinematic framing.

## 7. Photography

Photography should make the opportunity feel real.

Prefer:

- real or believable students;
- documentary/editorial framing;
- natural expressions;
- aspirational but grounded body language;
- authentic campus, city and learning environments;
- contextual Malaysian imagery only when the campaign is actually about Malaysia.

Avoid:

- obvious stock-photo poses;
- fake graduation composites;
- impossible campuses;
- excessive AI-perfect faces;
- generic handshake imagery;
- flags used as decoration without purpose;
- generated university logos or buildings presented as factual.

When AI imagery is used, it must remain an **unbranded visual layer**. Exact logos are composited afterward.

## 8. Graphic language

Approved supporting motifs include:

- the bench + rising-arrow movement;
- restrained journey/route cues;
- topographic/contour lines;
- subtle map references;
- clean directional lines;
- disciplined iconography;
- modular information blocks.

These should never compete with the logo or main message.

## 9. Depth and effects

Use depth to create hierarchy, not spectacle.

Allowed:

- restrained soft shadows;
- subtle glass/translucent surfaces where appropriate;
- cinematic light falloff;
- controlled gradients;
- slight depth separation;
- refined image masking.

Avoid:

- neon overload;
- glow around every element;
- 3D effects without narrative purpose;
- heavy bevels;
- random lens flares;
- game-like UI.

## 10. Campaign direction

### Education & Mobility

Primary feeling: **trust + possibility**.

Default to warm-white or clean light systems with black typography and green progress cues. Dark cinematic moments may be used for emotional storytelling, but detailed admissions information should remain highly readable.

Malaysia must read as a service destination, not as the identity of Last Bench itself.

### Events / community

Primary feeling: **belonging + momentum**.

Use stronger dark fields, bolder scale and more energetic motion while preserving the parent identity.

### Documents / applications

Primary feeling: **clarity + confidence**.

Use white/warm-white fields, disciplined typography, restrained green, clear tables and strong operational hierarchy.

## 11. Motion direction

Motion should explain:

- progression;
- route;
- transformation;
- hierarchy;
- transition between states.

Use:

- restrained reveal;
- directional line movement;
- subtle depth/parallax;
- controlled scroll transitions.

Avoid:

- bounce-heavy motion;
- decorative looping without purpose;
- excessive particles;
- interactions that reduce readability.

Always support reduced motion.

## 12. Social/poster system

A strong Last Bench poster should usually contain:

- exact transparent logo in the correct contrast variant;
- one dominant headline;
- one dominant visual;
- one clear CTA;
- supporting details grouped cleanly;
- enough negative space for the composition to breathe.

Do not create a second fake brand voice through invented slogans.

## 13. Partner / university campaigns

When a university or partner is featured:

- use the partner's verified logo only;
- do not redraw or approximate the partner identity;
- separate partner identity from Last Bench identity;
- preserve visual hierarchy so the viewer understands who the institution is and who the support/application partner is;
- verify campus/address/program information from source material before publication.

## 15. Art-direction release test

Before publishing any branded visual, verify:

- correct transparent logo variant for the background;
- green bench-arrow preserved;
- no logo plate;
- no generated/reconstructed logo;
- approved typography direction;
- hierarchy understandable within 3 seconds;
- readable at mobile scale;
- no invented factual claims;
- partner identity verified;
- saved/exported artifact inspected.

If any identity rule fails, the visual is not ready.
